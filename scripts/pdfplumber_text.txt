Quantitative and Qualitative Evaluative
Frameworks for the 2026 Disc Golf Pro
Tour Circuit
The professionalization of disc golf has reached a critical juncture where the selection and
rating of competitive venues and athletes require a multidimensional analytical approach. As
the Disc Golf Pro Tour Presented by Powerball® enters its 2026 season, the complexity of the
circuit—comprising 24 major coverage events across two continents—demands a standardized
metric system that aligns course difficulty with specific player abilities.
The Professional Evaluation Scale (1-5 Stars)
This framework is calibrated specifically for the 2026 Elite Series and Major standards. A 1-star
rating represents a professional-level baseline, while 5 stars denotes the absolute peak of that
attribute on the global tour.

Player Ability Index (PAI) Grounding
To ensure the Player Ability Index remains objective, abilities are mapped to professional
datasets:
1. Power: Based on Precise Power Index (PPI) and Max Distance data.
2. Accuracy: Based on Fairway Hit % and C1 in Regulation.
3. Recovery: Based on Scramble Rate % (Par saves after missed greens).
4. Resilience: Based on historical performance variance in extreme wind/heat.
5. Versatility: Based on FH/BH Usage Ratios and shot-shape mastery.17

High-Intensity Analysis: Matching Players to 2026
Venues
The 5-Star Distance Standard: Barela and Handley
On extreme 5-star distance tracks like Ivy Hill (12,000 ft) or New London Tech, athletes like
Anthony Barela (713ft recorded drive) and Holyn Handley provide a statistical "cheat code."
Their ability to reach par 5 landing zones in fewer strokes creates the scoring separation
necessary to beat "Accuracy Floor" players who may hit every gap but lack the raw distance to
birdie.
The Tech Gap: Robinson and Gannon
For venues like Parc des Familles and Shelton Springs, technical accuracy is the primary
bottleneck. Isaac Robinson and Missy Gannon lead the world in Fairway Hits % and Parked %
on constrained layouts. Robinson, despite his 1-star Versatility (Backhand-dominant), utilizes a
5-star Accuracy rating to outplay more versatile players on wooded corridor tracks.
Factual Resilience: The Wysocki Wind Standard
In volatile climates like Austin or OTB Open, Ricky Wysocki remains the historical outlier. His
standard deviation of scores on high-wind days is significantly lower than the field, grounded in
a "spin-putt" technique that is naturally more wind-resistant than "push-putt" variations.
Conclusion: Data-Driven Player Selection Foundations
By integrating these factual player abilities with the 2026 course ratings, fantasy users can
deploy a logic-based strategy. The IPAI (Integrated Player Ability Index) distinguishes
between "Floor" players (high Accuracy like Gannon) and "Ceiling" players (high Power like
Barela). For the 2026 season, success is a function of matching player intensity to venue
demands, providing the objective edge needed for a competitive fantasy sports application.
